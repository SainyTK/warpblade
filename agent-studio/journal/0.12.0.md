# 0.12.0 Gloam Shepherd: decision memo

Tonight is a big patch, with no day work, no new feedback and no commits since v0.11.2.
The baseline smoke check passed for all nine heroes with no page errors, so nothing is broken that must come first.

I chose a new boss for wave 15: the Gloam Shepherd, which replaces the Rift Brute.
Every era has its own leader except Nightfall.
The Rift Brute is a husk drawn larger with two moves, a swipe and a leap slam.
The Oakheart, the Bog Hulk, the Warden, the Maw and the Sovereign each have their own body and their own pattern.
A boss with two readable phases and a break between them is the Grand Chase feeling the charter asks for in combat, and it lands on pillar 1, combat first.

The Shepherd is a stooped, hooded herder of the husks with a bone crook and a lantern that holds a shard of moonlight.
In its first phase it reaps low across the floor, which you jump, and tolls its lantern down onto a ring it marks under you, which you step out of.
It also calls two husks out of the floor.
At just over half its HP it falls to one knee for a moment and takes extra damage from every blow.
Then it rises with the lantern burning violet: it reaps twice, lays a row of rings that burst in turn, and calls down shards of moonlight that are marked on the floor before they land.
Every attack shows itself before it hits, so a player who reads it takes nothing.

It keeps the mini boss rank, so wave 15 keeps its balance and Warden's End still means the Warden.
For pillar 4 it brings an achievement, Shepherd's End, and the game now counts kills per foe type so later bosses can have their own.

Class advancement is first on the roadmap, and I passed on it tonight.
It asks for a new combo part, special and look for nine heroes, which is more than one night.
Its unlock, a stage clear, is also out of reach in every bot run we measured, since all of them end by wave 5.
I rewrote it on the roadmap so a later big patch can ship the system with one or two heroes and a reachable unlock.

Run modifiers and the second round of achievements are mostly menus, and pillar 1 puts a fight ahead of a menu.
A new stage is too large for one night.
The wave 5 wall is still the biggest problem in the numbers, but the Oakheart was nerfed in 0.11.1 and the charter allows no second nerf within three patches.
It can be measured and tuned from 0.12.1.

The last big patch, 0.11.0, set the roster of nine heroes, so a new boss also rotates the headline away from heroes.
The developer measures the bots at wave 15 against the Brute before the change and against the Shepherd after, and keeps the fight's length within a quarter of the Brute's.

## What shipped

- The Gloam Shepherd as the Nightfall leader at wave 15, rank mini, 480 HP (the Brute had 420), 80 XP (70) and 10 coins (8).
- Phase 1: Reap (20 damage, 0.65 s wind-up, 54 px low sweep), Lantern Toll (18 px ring, 0.9 s wind-up, 22 damage) and Call the Flock (two husks, first at 6 s, then every 12 s, while fewer than two stand).
- The kneel at 55% HP: 1.6 s with no guard and no attacks, 1.5 times damage taken, then a 0.4 s rise into phase 2 that nothing can stagger, pin or knock down.
- Phase 2: Twin Reap (second wind-up 0.38 s), the Lantern Procession (three rings 10 px apart with a 1.1 s wind-up, bursting the hero's ring first, then the Shepherd's side, then the far side, 0.1 s apart) and Moonfall (five shards, 18 damage, each marked 0.55 s before it lands).
- The Rift Brute removed from the game: its type, behaviour, drawing, hits and coin entry.
- A shared `steady` hook on `EnemyKind` that drops any stun while a foe must not be knocked down, used by the kneel and the rise.
- Shepherd's End (60 coins, hidden until Night Watch), the `foeKills` goal kind, and kills counted per foe type in `TrackedStats.foes`; achievements go from 40 worth 2,900 coins to 41 worth 2,960.
- The README's era line and feature list, `docs/achievements.md` and `docs/meta-progress.md` updated for the Shepherd.
- QA rounds tightened the Shepherd after the first build: a steady kneel and rise, strikes snapped onto their first frame, a smaller Toll ring with a longer wind-up, and a Procession a hero can walk or jump out of.

Plan deviations, from the developer report: the Procession bursts the hero's own ring first and uses a 10 px gap, a 0.1 s beat and its own 1.1 s wind-up, where the plan said from the Shepherd's side outward, 30 px, 0.15 s and the Toll's 0.75 s.
That order is what lets a walk out work in either direction.
The phase 1 Toll is 18 px with a 0.9 s wind-up, where the plan said 22 px and 0.75 s.
The body glow uses `PAL.shadow[3]` at eye height -19, where the plan said `MOON_RIM` and -40.

## Runtime dependencies

None were added.
The patch does not change `package.json`.

## Balance

Time to kill the wave 15 leader at level 25 (skilled autopilot, hero kept alive, mean of nine heroes): 74.4 s for the Rift Brute, 71.0 s for the Gloam Shepherd, which is 5% faster, so its HP stays at 480.
Survival at wave 15, level 14 (skilled bot, mean game seconds): 28.1 s against the Brute, 35.0 s against the Shepherd, or 28.0 s without one long Brynn run of 90.8 s.
No level 14 bot reaches the second phase, so the roadmap keeps a tuning pass at a real run's level.
At level 25 Yun kills either leader in 2 to 3 s from the dev start build, which points at his start build and not the boss.

## QA verdict

QA's fourth round (qa-3) passed on HEAD 5dc0107.
Typecheck, lint and build pass, with only the existing Vite warning about a chunk over 700 kB.
The smoke check passes 140 of 140 checks for all nine heroes with no page errors.
Every Shepherd move, the kneel, the second phase and Shepherd's End were checked in play, and all nine heroes kill it with exactly one kneel and one rise.
In the Procession, walking away or toward after a 200 to 600 ms reaction and dodging lose nothing for every hero.
A v0.11.2 save loads with its coins, shop levels, bought hero, achievements and settings intact, and an empty foe count.
A live two-player co-op room saw both heroes hurt in both phases and the husks called, with no page errors.
The mash bot results are identical to the baseline.

QA left two minor issues.
With the rings 10 px apart and the hero's ring bursting first, the Procession reads at play scale as one wide violet ring with a widening burst, more than as three rings bursting in turn; a later art pass could make each burst easier to see.
Its jump window is narrow: jumps from about 700 to 950 ms after the rings appear clear it, and earlier or later jumps are hit, while walking and dodging work at every reaction time tested.
